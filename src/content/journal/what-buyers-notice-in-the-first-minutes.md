Buyers form an impression long before they reach the primary suite. What shapes that impression is rarely the feature list, it is the condition of the ordinary things they encounter first.

## The approach and the threshold

The drive, walkway, porch and front door are the first surfaces a buyer meets. Accumulated soil on flatwork, cobwebs at the eave, dust on the door and a tired entry mat all register before anyone speaks. A clean approach reads as a maintained home.

## Light, glass and air

Interior glass, sills and tracks affect how much light a room appears to hold. Clean glass photographs better and shows better. Still, heavy air in a closed house reads as neglect; a home that has been aired and freshened reads as lived in and cared for.

## Floors and grout lines

Flooring is the largest continuous surface in most homes, which makes it the most honest. Traffic lanes, edge shadowing along baseboards and uneven grout color tell a buyer how the house has been used. Restoration work here often changes the perceived age of a room more than paint does.

## The rooms that carry disproportionate weight

- Kitchen: cabinet faces, hardware, backsplash, sink and exterior appliance surfaces.
- Primary bath: glass, grout, fixtures, mirror edges and under-sink presentation.
- Entry and stairs: the two places nearly every visitor touches.
- Laundry and garage: often skipped, and often where buyers decide how the home was really kept.

## Presented, not merely cleaned

A market-ready home is arranged as well as cleaned: beds dressed evenly, towels aligned, counters edited down, cords managed, shades set to a consistent height, blinds level, scent neutral. None of this is staging in the furniture sense. It is presentation, and it is what photographs.

Sellers rarely regret preparing a home properly before photography. The window for a first impression does not reopen.
