A well-prepared guest room communicates something a welcome speech cannot: that someone thought about this visit in advance. The work is small, and the effect is disproportionate.

## The bed, first and carefully

Fresh linens, evenly made, with a second blanket folded within reach. Pillows in a mix of firmnesses if you have them. A clear surface on at least one nightstand, guests need somewhere to set a phone and a glass of water.

## The details guests notice quietly

- A reading light your guest can reach from the bed, and an outlet they do not have to move furniture to find.
- Space in the closet: empty hangers and a place to put an open suitcase.
- Fresh towels presented, plus a hand towel and washcloth, set out, not stored.
- Water, a glass, tissues and a small dish for jewelry or keys.
- Neutral scent, clear air, blinds and window coverings that actually darken the room.

## The guest bath

Guests forgive an unglamorous bathroom; they do not forgive an unprepared one. Clean glass and grout, a fresh soap, toilet tissue in view rather than hidden, a clear counter, and a small basket of basics, toothbrush, toothpaste, shampoo, remove the awkwardness of having to ask.

## One personal gesture

A short note, a few stems from the garden, a folded map of the neighborhood, or the wifi password written by hand. One human touch does more than a shelf of amenities.

## Before longer visits or holidays

For extended stays and holiday hosting, many households arrange a deeper reset in the week before arrival, glass, floors, textiles, guest spaces and secondary baths, so the final days are spent cooking and welcoming rather than catching up.

Preparing a guest room well is a small act of hospitality that guests remember far longer than the meal.
