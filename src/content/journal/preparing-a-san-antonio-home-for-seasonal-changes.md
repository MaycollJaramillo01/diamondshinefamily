San Antonio homes live through long heat, sudden storms, fine limestone dust, heavy pollen and short but real cold snaps. Each of those leaves a mark on materials, and each is easier to manage on a schedule than in reaction.

## Late winter into spring: dust and pollen

- Interior glass, sills and window tracks, the first places seasonal dust gathers.
- Vent faces, fan blades, ledges and fixture surfaces where fine dust settles.
- Entry mats and thresholds, which carry the majority of what comes indoors.
- Patios, walkways and outdoor seating brought back into use for the season.

## Summer: heat, traffic and hard water

- Fixtures, shower glass and sink surrounds, where mineral residue shows first.
- Entry paths and stair edges, which see the heaviest indoor traffic in a busy household.
- Soft surfaces after guests and pets, before soil is fully worked in.
- Shaded interior placement of textiles and rugs where strong light falls all afternoon.

## Autumn: hosting preparation

Autumn is when San Antonio homes get used hard: gatherings, out-of-town family, holiday cooking. A deeper reset before the season, kitchen detail, dining presentation, guest rooms, secondary baths, glass and floors, carries a household through months of hosting rather than one weekend.

## Winter: protection and review

- Interior air paths and dust-holding surfaces during closed-window months.
- Entry care during wet cold snaps, when soil tracks farther into the home.
- A condition review of restored floors, carpet and grout from earlier in the year.
- Next year's care rhythm confirmed before spring demand arrives.

## A note on local materials

Much of the region's beauty is in limestone, tile, stucco and stone, materials that reward gentle, appropriate care and show the effects of harsh methods quickly. Local homes also carry fine exterior dust indoors year-round, which is why entries, thresholds and floors deserve more frequent attention here than in many other markets.

Seasonal care is not a bigger effort, it is the same effort, arranged in the right order for the year we actually have.
