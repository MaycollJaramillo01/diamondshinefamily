A move begins well before the first box reaches the doorway. There are keys to collect, utilities to confirm, schedules to coordinate and decisions that will shape the first days in a new home. Preparing the interior is one part of that transition that benefits from a clear plan.

## Begin with the rooms you will use first

Think about the first evening: the bathroom you will use, the kitchen surfaces where you will set things down, and the bedroom where you hope to rest. These priorities help guide a service conversation. They sit alongside a complete, agreed scope and make the desired result easier to explain.

Share the home's approximate size, rooms, occupancy and condition. Clear photographs, when available, can help identify areas requiring a closer review. Some conditions or materials need an in-person assessment before the work can be priced or confirmed.

## Agree on the details that are easy to overlook

Cabinet interiors, appliances, interior windows, blinds and detached spaces should be discussed directly. A service name alone does not tell you whether every one of these items is included. A written proposal should state the areas covered, the important exclusions and any separately approved work.

If painters, movers or other trades will still be working, share that schedule. Dust or traffic after cleaning can change the result, and a home that is still being worked on may need a different sequence.

## Give the work a realistic window

The time a home needs depends on its size, condition, materials and agreed work. Ask how completion will be confirmed, especially if movers are expected later the same day. Avoid relying on an assumed finish time before the service team has assessed the plan.

Arrange authorized access and confirm working water and electricity. Keep entry codes and other sensitive instructions in a private channel. A photograph or public social post should never become the place where access information is stored.

## Share your care preferences

Tell the team about delicate finishes, known surface issues and any product or fragrance preferences. Fragrance enhancements are optional and should be discussed, including a preference for no added fragrance. The approach begins with the home and the people who will live in it.

## A short checklist before service

- Confirm which rooms and specific details are included in the written scope.
- Coordinate the service with other trades and the arrival of movers.
- Arrange authorized access, working water and electricity.
- Share material concerns and product or fragrance preferences.
- Agree on how completion and any remaining items will be communicated.

## Leave room for the next chapter

Once the first service is complete, review the work and any remaining items. If recurring care would support your routine, that conversation can follow your experience. The right frequency depends on use, priorities, scope and the available schedule.
