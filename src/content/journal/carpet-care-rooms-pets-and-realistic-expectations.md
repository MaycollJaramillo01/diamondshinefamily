A request for six rooms tells us where a carpet conversation begins. It does not yet tell us how those rooms are used, how much carpet is accessible or which areas need closer attention. A considered quote turns a room count into a clear plan, so the homeowner and the team arrive with the same expectations.

## Describe the spaces, not just the number

List each bedroom, living room, office, hallway and staircase you want assessed. Point out oversized spaces, connecting areas and closets. Share approximate dimensions if available, along with an overview photo of each room.

An empty residence and a furnished home can require different coordination. Tell us whether anyone is living in the property and which areas will be clear. Do not assume furniture moving or every adjoining area is included; have those details confirmed in the written scope.

## Separate everyday soil from pet concerns

Tell us about known accidents, recurring odors and the locations that concern you, even when a mark is no longer visible. This allows us to discuss whether targeted pet treatment should be evaluated alongside the carpet service.

The Carpet and Rug Institute notes that urine can affect carpet dyes and that some color changes may be permanent. It also describes how repeated contamination can extend into the backing, cushion or subfloor. Cleaning the visible surface does not automatically resolve every underlying issue.

## Give the team a short history

- When the carpet was last professionally cleaned, if known.
- Products or home machines already used on the problem areas.
- Any known water damage, recurring pet accidents or persistent odors.
- Areas that must remain accessible during the visit and any upcoming move or furniture delivery.

## Ask for a scope you can recognize

A useful proposal names the areas, identifies any separately assessed treatment and explains the access assumptions. It should leave you able to point to each included space without interpreting vague package language.

The room count is a quoting input, not a promise of identical results in every room. Age, condition, previous treatment and the nature of each mark affect what can reasonably be expected. Ask the team to distinguish what may improve from what appears to be wear or lasting damage.

## Leave room for drying and review

Discuss the practical drying plan before the appointment, especially if the home is occupied. Follow the instructions provided for your carpet and the method used, including when people, pets and furniture can return to the area.

After the work, review any remaining concerns with the team. If a mark or odor returns, report where and when it appeared. That information is more useful than applying additional products without a clear plan.
